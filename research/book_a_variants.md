# Book A variants: results (2026-10-02)

Rules, variants and pass bar: `book_a_variants_prereg.md` (merged in PR #44 before the run). Script:
`book_a_variants.py`. Output: `book_a_variants_out/summary.json` (trades.csv and daily.csv are regenerated by the
script in about 15 minutes; they are not committed).

> **Pricing note (2026-10-07; relabel only, nothing re-run).** The 2005-2020 tables below (sections 1-3 and 5)
> come from a **cheap-option model**. `book_a_variants.py` fed IV = prior VIX × 0.80 to `backtest.ModelQuotes` on
> a calendar clock (time left ÷ 365 days), not on the trading-day clock HANDOFF section 7 describes and the B/D
> research uses (sd over the time left = 0.80 × VIX/√252 × √(RTH share left + 15/390)). At S = 765, VIX 16,
> 09:30 ET that priced the ATM call at $1.10 instead of $2.51, the 1-OTM call at $0.65 instead of $2.04 and the
> 3-OTM call at $0.17 instead of $1.29 (0.44×, 0.32×, 0.13×), so A's calls carried about twice the real leverage,
> more for OTM strikes. The "1,409 stops inside the first minute" and "a 1-OTM call loses 20% on a move of about
> 0.04%" (section 3) are cheap-model figures; on the trading-day clock a 1-OTM call needs about 0.08-0.12%
> (10:00-12:00 CT) to lose 20%. The 60-session SIP replay quoted here (`iex_vs_sip.py`, −$2,303) used the same
> calendar clock. **The real-quote holdout (section 4b: ThetaData quotes, `book_a_holdout.py`) does not use the
> model and is unaffected**: the −$24/day baseline and the −35% stop decision stand. `ModelQuotes` now defaults to
> the trading-day clock; `book_a_variants.py` pins `clock="calendar"` so these tables still reproduce.

## Bottom line

- **Holdout verdict (real quotes, 2026-10-02): nothing passes, and A loses on real option prices too.** On
  1,348 sessions of real SPY 0DTE quotes (2020-06 to 2026-09) the baseline loses $24 a day (t −6.4, PF 0.74),
  every year negative. The wider −35% stop is the best variant, +$10 a day better than the baseline (paired
  t +7.0), but still loses $14 a day. See section 4b.
- **No variant passes, and the baseline loses.** In-sample (2005-14) every variant has a negative mean daily
  P&L, so none clears the bar. Book A as built loses about $7 a day in 2005-14 and $14 a day in 2015-20 (t −1.7 and
  −2.0) on 1-minute data with modeled option prices.
- **More trades means more losses.** Every way of adding entries keeps the loss per trade about where it is, so
  the extra trades add to the loss: puts (5.2 trades a day, −$21/day), 15m-only filter (4.8 a day, −$31/day).
- **Removing the RSI cap (v1) is the one harmless change.** It adds about 0.4 trades a day (+15%) at the same
  loss per day as the baseline (paired difference t +0.13 in-sample, −0.33 out-of-sample). It would have let A
  trade this morning, but it doesn't make A profitable.
- **Slower exits (v4) make A worse, not better** (paired t −6.1 in-sample, −2.1 out-of-sample). Without the 1m
  cross-back exit, more trades ride down to the −20% stop.
- **The real problem is the −20% stop, not the exits Evan asked about.** It ends 42% of baseline trades and 1,409
  of them inside the first minute. See section 3.

## 2. Why mid and taker match

The model's option spread is 1c under $1 and 2c under $3, so mid ±1c lands on the touch for most of A's
contracts. Real SPY 0DTE spreads near the money are about that tight, so costs are not what sinks A here; the
signal is.

A 1-OTM 0DTE call loses 20% on a move of about 0.04% in SPY, which a single minute often covers. So the −20%
stop fires on noise right after entry, and that, more than the cross back, is what turns A negative.

Careful reading: the hold-time table is partly circular (a trade that goes against you gets stopped quickly, a
trade that works gets held), so it does not prove that a wider stop would make money. v4 shows that just
holding longer, with the same stop, loses more. A wider stop, a stop on SPY's price instead of the premium, or a
less jumpy contract (ATM/ITM, or a debit spread) are new ideas from these results. Under the pre-registration
(section 4) they would need their own pre-registration and could be tested only on the holdout, never on
2005-2020 again.

## 4. Holdout on the Mac (pre-registration section 5)

No variant passed, so the only holdout runs the pre-registration calls for are v5 (SWING only) on real SIP
ticks, plus the baseline on real quotes, which gives A its first real-quote number. v1 is added for information
only (it failed the bar, but it is the change that fixes days like 2026-10-02). `book_a_holdout.py` does both:

- `quotes`: SPY SIP 1-minute bars 2020-06 onward with ThetaData's real 0DTE quotes, 1m trigger.
- `ticks`: the 60 cached SIP sessions (Jul-Sep 2026) with real 144-tick bars and ThetaData quotes, SWING and SCALP.

### 4a. First holdout run was invalid (stale quotes), fixed 2026-10-02

The first real-quote run (Mac, 2020-06-01 to 2026-09-25, 1,348 sessions, commit ca1b901) showed the baseline at
+$112 a day, PF 5.9, t 22.5, with 80% of trading days green in every year. That is a timing bug, not an edge. The
script priced each option at the previous minute's ThetaData row, which is up to two minutes older than the SPY
price that triggered the trade, so every entry on an up-move bought at the pre-move price and every exit after a
drop sold at the pre-drop price. Reproduced on synthetic data (S&P 1-minute bars with model quotes stamped like
ThetaData's 1-minute rows): the same rules made +$221 a day with the stale rows and −$41 a day with continuous
model quotes. The fix keeps the previous minute's row (never a future quote) but moves its mid by delta × ΔSPY +
gamma × ΔSPY² / 2 since that row, reading delta and gamma off the neighbouring strikes in the same row. On the
synthetic data the fixed script gives −$44 and −$37 a day (start- and end-of-minute stamps) against the −$41
reference. `research/tests/test_book_a_holdout.py` pins it. The invalid results were removed from the branch; the
rerun uses the same variants and pass bar. The variants' relative ranks in the invalid run are not used.

What this says:
- Book A as built loses on every data set tried: modeled options 2005-2020, real quotes 2020-2026, and real
  ticks Jul-Sep 2026 (agrees with the earlier 60-day SIP replay, −$2,303).
- Removing the RSI cap (v1) adds trades and adds losses on real quotes (−$4 a day vs the baseline, t −2.6).
- The stop is the right place to look: the −35% stop cuts the loss by about 40% on six years of real quotes.
  It does not show up on the 60 tick sessions, which are too few to tell (t −0.2). It still loses money, so
  under the pre-registration it fails.
- Dropping SCALP (v5) cuts the tick loss by about 60%, in line with the SIP replay that found SCALP the bigger
  loser, but 60 days can't confirm it (t +1.2) and SWING alone still loses.
- The holdout has now been used. Any further change to A's signal would need data none of these runs has seen
  (paper days from here on).

## 5. Caveats

- Option prices come from Black-Scholes with IV = prior VIX × 0.80 and a fixed intraday IV; real 0DTE IV moves
  during the day. That IV ran on a calendar clock, so 0DTE calls priced at about half of real (the
  cheap-option model; see the pricing note at the top). 1-minute bars are replayed as open, low, high, close on up bars (open, high, low, close on down
  bars), so a stop inside a bar's range always fires first.
- No 144t, so no SCALP trades (the SIP replay found SCALP the bigger loser). No event blackouts (no historical
  calendar). S&P 500 CFD prices re-based to SPY 765, not SPY itself.
- The engine sends only down crosses to exit plans, so live puts would never get a cross-back exit. The script
  mirrors this for v2. If puts are ever adopted, `engine.py` needs the same fix.
